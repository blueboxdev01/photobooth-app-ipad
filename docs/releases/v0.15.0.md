# v0.15.0-ipad — It stops getting slower, and both screens agree

**Download `photobooth-v0.15.0-win-x64.zip`, unzip, run `Photobooth.Server.exe`.**
Then open <http://localhost:5000/operator>.

> The version at the foot of the rail reads **`0.15.0-ipad`**. Please quote it
> with any result.

## The booth no longer slows down as the night goes on

Three separate things were making a session take longer the later it got. All
three are fixed, and the last one was the real culprit.

**The photos were being decoded over and over.** Building the strip and the GIF
opened each source photo once for every frame it appeared in, always at the
camera's full 24-megapixel resolution, to fill a slot a few hundred pixels
wide. Now each photo is read once, at a size that suits what it is being drawn
into. A session went from **6.7 seconds to 2.5**.

**The booth was re-reading every finished session.** Each delivery poll opened
and parsed the record of every session on disk — so the cost grew all evening.
Two hundred sessions went from **35ms to 11ms** per poll.

**The watch folder was the big one.** Nobody empties it between guests, so by
the end of an event it holds every photo taken. The booth was re-examining all
of them every two seconds, and putting each one through the full "is this file
finished writing" wait before rejecting it on a timestamp. In testing, a single
leftover photo was examined **125 times**, and a genuine new photo had to queue
behind the whole backlog.

That is what "the countdown is late and the strip is slow, but only after a
while" was. In one test run before the fix, a session never left the review
screen at all.

Afterwards, ten sessions in a row with 48 leftovers in the folder: every one
finished in about **0.6 seconds**, flat, no stalls.

## The two screens now count the same seconds

The operator showing 3 while the display showed 6 was a clock difference. The
booth decides when the countdown ends and sends that as an absolute time; each
screen then worked out "how long is that from now?" using its own clock. The
laptop is the booth, so it always agreed with itself. The iPad has its own
clock, and one a few seconds out counted a few seconds differently.

Every update now carries the booth's own time alongside the deadline, and each
screen corrects for the gap. **You do not need to fix the iPad's clock** — it
works however wrong it is.

## Guests save photos to their camera roll, without the zip

Tapping a photo used to download it. On an iPhone a download goes to **Files**,
not Photos — so a guest following the instructions ended up with their pictures
somewhere they would never think to look, and had to unpack a zip to get there.

Now a tapped photo opens full screen, and a **touch and hold** offers
**Add to Photos** on iOS or **Download image** on Android. Both put it straight
in the camera roll, named after the session. The page says so on it.

**Being straight about this: it is two gestures, not one.** A single-tap share
button needs a secure connection, and the guest page is deliberately plain so
the iPad can fetch its certificate over it. Two gestures to the right place
beats one gesture to the wrong one.

The zip is still there, below the photos, for anyone who wants everything at
once.

## What to look at

1. **Run a lot of sessions.** Ten or more, without clearing the output or watch
   folders. The tenth should feel exactly like the first — that is the whole
   point of this release. If the booth gets sluggish, that is the result worth
   sending back.
2. **Watch both screens during a countdown.** They should show the same number.
   Try it without correcting the iPad's clock.
3. **On a phone, save a photo to the camera roll.** Tap it, hold it, Add to
   Photos. Then check it is actually in Photos.
4. **Then try the GIF the same way** and confirm it still moves once saved.

## Everything else

Unchanged from v0.14.0: the MemoStills console, the Guest Gallery page, the
looping GIF, guest photos over your own wifi, the address picker, and the iPad
guest display.

Still outstanding, unchanged: a custom backdrop set in Settings does not reach
the iPad, which keeps its default dark background.
