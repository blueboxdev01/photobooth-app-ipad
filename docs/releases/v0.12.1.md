# v0.12.1-ipad — The restart banner can now actually clear

**Download `photobooth-v0.12.1-win-x64.zip`, unzip, run `Photobooth.Server.exe`.**
Then open <http://localhost:5000/operator>.

**If you are already testing v0.12.0, you do not have to stop.** Everything in
that build works. Only the status line was wrong. Pick this up when convenient.

> The version at the foot of the rail reads **`0.12.1-ipad`**. Please quote it
> with any result.

## The fix

A tester pressed **Serve the display to the network**, restarted as instructed,
and Setup still said a restart was needed. Restarting again did not help, and
never would have — **that banner could not clear.**

Guest photos were switched on the whole time. The ports were open and serving.
Only the thing reporting it was wrong, which is the worst shape a bug can take:
the person testing has no way to tell the feature is already running, and
reasonably concludes it is not.

**What was wrong:** the booth worked that setting out twice — once for opening
the ports, once for reporting its state — and only the first had the operator's
choice applied to it. There is one answer now.

## If you are on v0.12.0

Skip step 7 of the test guide. Guest photos are already on after your restart,
whatever the banner says. Go straight to Stage 3 and carry on; nothing else in
the guide is affected.

## Also in this build

The printable test guide now ships **inside the ZIP** as
`Photobooth-v0.12.1-Test-Guide.pdf`, alongside `GUEST-PHOTOS.md` and
`TESTING-GUEST-PHOTOS.md`, so the build and its instructions travel together.

Stage 2 of the guide now tells you to confirm the section shows **addresses and
a certificate fingerprint**, rather than resting on the banner alone — and says
what an older build's stuck banner means, so nobody loses an afternoon to it
twice.

## Everything else

Unchanged from v0.12.0: guests download their own photos over your wifi, the
address picker, the iPad guest display, and the whole of v0.10.0 underneath.

`GUEST-PHOTOS.md` remains the setup and troubleshooting reference. The network
advice has not changed: **a travel router with nothing in its WAN port**, so
there is no internet on it for guests to reach.
