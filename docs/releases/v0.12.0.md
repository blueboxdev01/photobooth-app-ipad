# v0.12.0-ipad — Guests download their own photos, over your own wifi

**Download `photobooth-v0.12.0-win-x64.zip`, unzip, run `Photobooth.Server.exe`.**
Nothing to install — no .NET, no Node. Exactly as before.

Then open <http://localhost:5000/operator>.

## What is new since v0.11.0

**Guests can take their own photos away, without an account.** At the end of a
session the guest screen shows two codes: one to join your wifi, one to open
their own photos. The page has the strip, every photo, and a download-all zip.

No Google account, no cloud, **no internet at all**. This replaced Drive after
the booth's Google account was suspended and took every guest's link with it.
Nothing here can be suspended.

**Google Drive delivery is switched off** in this build for the same reason. The
code is still there; the switch is off.

## The one setting that matters most

**Setup → Guest photos over your wifi → Guests reach the booth at.**

A booth laptop is routinely on more than one network — the guests' router and
your own wifi — and **only one of those addresses is reachable from a guest's
phone**. The photos code can carry only one of them.

Earlier builds picked whichever address Windows happened to list first. That is
not a choice, and when it is wrong the code scans perfectly, the page never
loads, and nothing on screen says why. Now you pick, from a list that names the
adapter beside each address, and the booth says out loud when it is guessing
between several.

It also refuses to advertise an address the machine does not have — a choice
saved at the last venue is a dead address at this one, and Setup says so rather
than quietly using it.

## The network you need

**A travel router with nothing plugged into its WAN port.** Plug the booth
laptop into a LAN port by cable, and let guests join its wifi.

That router has no uplink, so **there is no internet on it for guests to use** —
which is the point. The booth laptop can be on your own wifi at the same time
without becoming a bridge between the two; Windows does not route between
adapters unless Mobile Hotspot turns it on.

Two things that will waste your afternoon if nobody says them:

- **Not the laptop's Mobile Hotspot.** It shares whatever internet the laptop
  has, and it **caps at 8 devices** — and phones stay joined long after their
  owner walks away.
- **Not the router's "guest network" mode.** It reads backwards: *guest* means
  isolated from the other devices, and the booth is a device your guests need to
  reach. Put them on the main wifi with client isolation **off**.

## How to test it

**`TESTING-GUEST-PHOTOS.md`, in the ZIP.** Three parts:

| Part | Needs | Proves |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Nothing but the laptop, ~10 min | The booth offers real addresses and puts the one you chose in the code |
| **B** | A travel router and one phone | A guest gets their photos, and **cannot** get your internet |
| **C** | The same, plus willingness to break things | The warnings actually fire |

**Please do not skip Part C.** Every problem this feature has had so far was a
silent failure — something that looked fine and was not. Part C causes each of
them on purpose so you learn to recognise the symptom in thirty seconds instead
of twenty minutes.

`GUEST-PHOTOS.md`, also in the ZIP, is the setup and troubleshooting reference.

## Known limitations, stated plainly

- **The link dies when the guest leaves your wifi.** This is delivery on the
  spot. A guest who gets home and realises they saved nothing has to ask you.
- **Phones complain that the network has no internet.** iOS shrugs and stays.
  **Android may switch itself back to mobile data** and take the page with it —
  so the sign needs to say *"your phone will say there's no internet; stay
  connected."*
- **Venue wifi will not work.** It nearly always separates its devices from each
  other, which blocks this completely. That is what the router is for.
